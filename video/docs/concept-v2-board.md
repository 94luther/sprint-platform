# Sprint concept film v2 board: "Show the machine"
44 seconds, 1920x1080, 30fps, 1320 frames. Owner goal, stated verbatim and binding on
every reviewer: show what the product does, how it looks on a customer's phone, how it
looks on another company's website, WhatsApp ordering, and the value proposition, so a
viewer understands what this thing is. Cinematic craft serves the demonstration.

Beat 1 (0 to 3s): ink ground, one line, huge: "Everything in Gaborone gets a Sprint
delivery option." Hard cut.

Beat 2 (3 to 10s), THE APP: a phone showing real app screens in sequence: catalog with
search and category chips, a merchant menu with the cart, then checkout with itemised
fees (P100 items, P15 delivery, P5 service, P120 total, the customer's money labelled).
Type: "Your customers order in the app." Caption chips: "Search the whole city" and
"Fees shown before you pay."

Beat 3 (10 to 17s), THE WEBSITE: a fictional bakery site (Thato's Bakery, Gaborone,
clearly invented) in a browser mock with one Sprint green button: "Order. Sprint
delivers." A cursor clicks; Sprint checkout slides over the shop's own page with the
payment rails (Orange Money, MyZaka, Smega, Cash). Type: "Or on any shop's own website."
Caption: "The shop keeps its customer. Sprint does the delivery."

Beat 4 (17 to 24s), WHATSAPP: a chat mock: customer "2 loaves and milk to Block 8";
reply order card "Thato's Bakery, P57 total, deliver 25 min" with Orange Money and Cash
chips; tap highlights Orange Money; reply "Paid. Track your rider:" with a link chip.
Type: "Or straight in WhatsApp."

Beat 5 (24 to 31s), THE TRACKING: real arrival footage dimmed behind a phone showing the
live glowing map with the courier card. Captions: "Live map" and "Arrives by 14:35 or we
call you". Type: "They watch it come."

Beat 6 (31 to 37s), THE MONEY: the customer's P90 splits into three labelled rows:
P67.50 to the merchant, P16.20 to the rider, P6.30 to Sprint. Sunset still small in a
corner card. Type: "The money splits itself, fairly."

Beat 7 (37 to 44s), ONE MACHINE and CLOSE: five nodes (The app, The web, The shop's
site, WhatsApp, Corporate) draw lines into one Sprint node with the cheetah mark.
Type: "Five ways in. One machine." Then the end card: logo, skewed SPRINT lockup with
the orange slash, "Built for Gaborone. Moving with you." and small "Gaborone, delivered."

Constraints: real screens never stretched; zero em or en dashes on screen; no real brand
but Sprint's own; money always labelled with whose it is; Veo footage seasons beats 5
and 6 only; type enters with a small rise and never overlaps UI.

Gemini (Flash-Lite, provisional) findings on v2.1:
G1 Cut 47s to 30s: kill beat 1 (open directly on the bakery site), kill beat 5
   THE APP entirely (it contradicts "the shop keeps its customer": you promise
   infrastructure then advertise your own marketplace mall), money beat to 2s,
   close to 3s.
G2 Audience whiplash: B2B open then consumer middle weakens both pitches. If the
   shop owner is the buyer, frame every beat around their benefit.
G3 Merchant beat unconvincing as a personal-phone ping: owners fear missed
   notifications and extra hardware. "Orders hit your till" beats "Orders land
   on your phone". Land the payout in the same beat as the Accept.
G4 Weakest beat: THE MONEY, six seconds of static accounting kills momentum.
G5 Confusion risk: merchant phone to WhatsApp jump reads as random screens;
   channels need a visual link to the same machine.
G6 Biggest overlooked thing (standing question): PHYSICAL REALITY. The film is
   all glowing rectangles; a shop owner trusts a branded rider and a handoff,
   not software. "You have built a software ad for a physical logistics
   product. Show the physical machine."
Referee note on G1/G2: cutting THE APP wholesale conflicts with the owner's
binding goal (customer's phone must be shown). The tension is real though;
candidate resolution is a merchant-first 30s primary cut and the fuller 47s as
the leadership/website version, to be put to the owner after the deep re-run.
